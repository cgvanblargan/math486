    Exercises 1.6 Exercises

    Homework exercises for chapter 1.
    🔗

    1. The Pay Increase.

    Three elected county commissioners, \(A\text{,}\) \(B\text{,}\) and \(C\text{,}\) hold a public vote on whether to give themselves a pay increase. The rules of the vote are as follows.
    🔗
    • Each commissioner votes yes (\(y\)) or no (\(n\)), and the measure passes if at least two of the three vote yes.
      🔗
      🔗
    • Votes are announced in order: \(A\) votes first; \(B\) observes \(A\)’s vote and then announces; \(C\) observes both votes and then announces.
      🔗
      🔗
    • If the measure passes, each commissioner receives a benefit of \(v\text{,}\) whether or not that commissioner voted for it.
      🔗
      🔗
    • Any commissioner who votes yes pays a political cost \(c\text{,}\) and pays it whether or not the measure passes.
      🔗
      🔗
    • Both \(v\) and \(c\) are positive, with \(v > c\text{.}\) Benefits and costs are additive, so \(A\)’s payoff is \(A\)’s benefit minus \(A\)’s cost.
      🔗
      🔗
    The extensive form game is shown below, with payoffs listed in the order \((A, B, C)\text{.}\) Four of the terminal nodes are marked \((?,?,?)\text{.}\)
    🔗

    (a)

    Determine the payoffs at the four terminal nodes marked \((?,?,?)\text{.}\) Include a sketch or screenshot of the game tree with the correct payoffs written in. A verbal description of the actions is not a substitute for the sketch.
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    How many strategies does each commissioner have? Report your answers as \(|S_A|\text{,}\) \(|S_B|\text{,}\) and \(|S_C|\) (these represent the size of each player’s strategy set). You do not need to write out the strategy sets themselves, and you do not need to write an explanation, but show enough work that it is clear how you counted.
    🔗
    🔗

    (c)

    Write out one of \(C\)’s strategies, and describe what it means in words. Your description should be in complete sentences, and it should make clear why the thing you wrote down is a strategy rather than a single vote.
    🔗
    Hint.
    There are four nodes where \(C\) is called on to vote, one for each way the first two votes could have gone. A strategy has to say something about all four, including the ones that won’t be reached.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (d)

    Use backward induction to analyze the game and predict the outcome. Show the steps graphically, in the style of the book’s Figures 1.4 and 1.5. Do not write out explanations of each step. Clearly indicate the path through the tree that play will follow.
    🔗
    🔗

    (e)

    According to your backward induction analysis, what is \(C\)’s rational strategy? What you report here must be a strategy for \(C\text{.}\) You may want to revisit the definition of a strategy before answering.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    2. Four Players and an Entry Fee.

    Four players, \(P_1\text{,}\) \(P_2\text{,}\) \(P_3\text{,}\) and \(P_4\text{,}\) play the following game.
    🔗
    • Entry fee. Before play begins, every player pays a $1 entry fee.
      🔗
      🔗
    • Order of play. Players move one at a time in the order \(P_1\text{,}\) \(P_2\text{,}\) \(P_3\text{,}\) \(P_4\text{.}\) A player moves only if every player before her has chosen to continue.
      🔗
      🔗
    • The two choices. On her turn, a player either (E)nds the game or (C)ontinues.
      🔗
      🔗
    • Ending. A player who plays E pays an additional $1. The game ends immediately, and each of the other three players receives $11 ($10 plus the $1 entry fee back). The player who ended the game receives nothing.
      🔗
      🔗
    • Continuing. A player who plays C pays nothing, and play passes to the next player.
      🔗
      🔗
    • Reaching the end. If \(P_4\) plays C, the game simply ends with no further payments, and all four players are out their $1 entry fee.
      🔗
      🔗

    (a)

    Sketch the extensive form of this game. Be careful to account for every payment at each outcome: all four players pay the $1 entry fee before play begins, a player who ends the game pays a further $1, and the other three each collect $11. Report each player’s payoff as the net amount she gains or loses over the whole game.
    🔗
    Hint.
    The tree is a single chain of four decision nodes. Each node has one branch ending the game and one continuing, so there are five terminal nodes in all.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Determine the backward induction solution.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    3. A Three Player Game.

    Consider the following extensive form game with three players. Payoffs are listed in the usual way, so that \((p_1, p_2, p_3)\) means \(p_1\) is the payoff to player 1, \(p_2\) the payoff to player 2, and \(p_3\) the payoff to player 3.
    🔗

    (a)

    For each player \(i\text{,}\) determine \(|S_i|\) (that is the size of the strategy set for player i). You do not need to write out the strategy sets. An explanation isn’t necessary, but include enough work that it’s clear how you determined each size.
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    For player 3, describe in words what is meant by the strategy \(s_3 = x\,b\text{,}\) where player 3’s two decision nodes are taken in left-to-right order. Write your description in complete sentences.
    🔗
    🔗

    (c)

    Use backward induction to analyze the game. Indicate graphically the path of play your analysis predicts.
    🔗
    🔗

    (d)

    According to your backward induction analysis, what strategy will each player use? Make sure that what you report for each player really is a strategy.
    🔗
    🔗

    (e)

    Identify at least one outcome that all three players would strictly prefer to the outcome reached by backward induction. That is, every player must be better off, not merely no worse off.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    4. When Backward Induction Fails.

    We saw that the backward induction procedure can break down when, at some step, two moves give the deciding player the same best payoff. In both games below, player 2 is indifferent between her two moves, so the procedure breaks down in both. The two games differ in whether that breakdown actually costs us a prediction. In both trees, payoffs are listed in the order \((p_1, p_2)\text{.}\)
    🔗

    (a)

    Carry out backward induction on Game A as far as you can. At which node does the procedure break down, and why? Then explain, in complete sentences, why the breakdown leaves player 1 unable to decide between his two moves.
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Now turn to Game B. Show that backward induction breaks down at player 2’s node for the same reason it did in Game A.
    🔗
    🔗

    (c)

    Even though the procedure breaks down, player 1 has a clearly rational choice in Game B. Identify it, and explain in complete sentences why player 1 can make that choice without knowing what player 2 would do.
    🔗
    Hint.
    Compare player 1’s payoff from \(a\) with every payoff player 1 could end up with after playing \(b\text{,}\) rather than with the single payoff backward induction would have assigned to player 2’s node.
