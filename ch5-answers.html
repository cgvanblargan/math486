    Exercises 5.8 Answers to Ch. 5 Exercises

    Answers to the exercises of this chapter. Click an exercise to open it, click a part to open that part, then click Solution to reveal the answer.
    🔗
    These are the answers to the questions, not fully worked solutions. Enough is shown that you can tell whether your own work got to the correct conclusion.
    🔗

    1. Computing a Mixed Equilibrium.

    (a) The mixed strategy Nash equilibrium.

    Find a mixed strategy Nash equilibrium in which both players use both of their strategies.
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(\left(\frac{1}{5}a + \frac{4}{5}b,\ \frac{3}{4}x + \frac{1}{4}y\right)\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b) Expected payoffs.

    What is each player’s expected payoff at this equilibrium?
    🔗
    Solution.
    Player 1 gets \(\frac{3}{2}\) and Player 2 gets \(\frac{28}{5}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    2. A Symmetric \(3 \times 3\) Game.

    (a) The system of equations.

    Set up a system of linear equations whose solution gives a symmetric Nash equilibrium \((\sigma, \sigma)\) with all three strategies active.
    🔗
    Solution.
    Writing \(\sigma = xa + yb + zc\text{,}\) Player 1’s three strategies must earn the same payoff against \(\sigma\text{,}\) and the probabilities must add to \(1\text{:}\)
    \begin{align*} \pi_1(a, \sigma) \amp = 2x + 6y + 5z\\ \pi_1(b, \sigma) \amp = 3x + 3y + 9z\\ \pi_1(c, \sigma) \amp = 4x + 4y + 7z \end{align*}
    So the system is
    \begin{align*} 2x + 6y + 5z = 3x + 3y + 9z \amp = 4x + 4y + 7z\\ x + y + z \amp = 1 \end{align*}
    (Using \(z = 1 - x - y\) instead gives two equations in \(x\) and \(y\text{.}\))
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b) The symmetric Nash equilibrium.

    Solve the system and state the Nash equilibrium \((\sigma, \sigma)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    \((\sigma, \sigma)\) with \(\sigma = \frac{1}{6}a + \frac{1}{2}b + \frac{1}{3}c\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    3. Active and Inactive Strategies.

    (a) All three strategies active.

    Show that there is no mixed strategy Nash equilibrium in which all three of Player 1’s strategies are active.
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(\pi_1(a, \sigma_2) = \pi_1(b, \sigma_2)\) requires \(q = \frac{1}{2}\text{,}\) but \(\pi_1(a, \sigma_2) = \pi_1(c, \sigma_2)\) requires \(q = \frac{2}{3}\text{.}\) Both can’t hold, so there is no such equilibrium.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b) \(a\) and \(b\) active.

    Find a mixed strategy Nash equilibrium with \(a\) and \(b\) active in \(\sigma_1\text{,}\) \(c\) inactive, and both \(x\) and \(y\) active in \(\sigma_2\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    • \(\left(\frac{3}{4}a + \frac{1}{4}b,\ \frac{1}{2}x + \frac{1}{2}y\right)\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    • Inequality condition: \(\pi_1(a, \sigma_2) = \pi_1(b, \sigma_2) = 4\) and \(\pi_1(c, \sigma_2) = \frac{7}{2} \leq 4\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (c) \(a\) and \(c\) active.

    Show that there is no mixed strategy Nash equilibrium with \(a\) and \(c\) active in \(\sigma_1\) and \(b\) inactive.
    🔗
    Solution.
    The equality condition gives \(q = \frac{2}{3}\text{.}\) Then \(\pi_1(a, \sigma_2) = \pi_1(c, \sigma_2) = \frac{10}{3}\text{,}\) but \(\pi_1(b, \sigma_2) = \frac{13}{3} \gt \frac{10}{3}\text{,}\) so the inequality condition fails.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    4. A Two-Stage Prisoner’s Dilemma.

    (a) The missing payoffs.

    Find the payoffs for \((D, T)\) and \((T, D)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    \((D, T)\) gives \((5, -1)\) and \((T, D)\) gives \((-1, 5)\text{.}\) In stage 1 the defector gets \(5\) and the tit for tat player gets \(-1\text{;}\) in stage 2 both play \(d\) and get \(0\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b) Pure strategy Nash equilibria.

    Find all pure strategy Nash equilibria.
    🔗
    Solution.
    \((D, D)\) and \((T, T)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (c) The equality condition.

    Show that the equality condition holds for every \(p\) and \(q\) in \((0, 1)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(\pi_1(C, \sigma_2) = \pi_1(T, \sigma_2) = 6\) and \(\pi_2(\sigma_1, C) = \pi_2(\sigma_1, T) = 6\text{,}\) no matter what \(p\) and \(q\) are.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (d) The inequality condition.

    Find exactly which values of \(p\) and \(q\) make \((\sigma_1, \sigma_2)\) a Nash equilibrium, and interpret.
    🔗
    Solution.
    • \(\pi_1(D, \sigma_2) = 5 + 5q \leq 6\) gives \(q \leq \frac{1}{5}\text{,}\) and likewise \(\pi_2(\sigma_1, D) = 5 + 5p \leq 6\) gives \(p \leq \frac{1}{5}\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    • So the Nash equilibria are exactly the profiles with \(0 \lt p \leq \frac{1}{5}\) and \(0 \lt q \leq \frac{1}{5}\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    • In words: a player can use always cooperate at most \(\frac{1}{5}\) of the time. Any more, and the other player does better by switching to always defect.
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    5. A Simple Poker Game, Revisited.

    (a) The payoff functions.

    Find \(\pi_1(p, q)\) and \(\pi_2(p, q)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    \begin{align*} \pi_1(p, q) \amp = 2p(1-q) + q(1-p)\\ \pi_2(p, q) \amp = -2p(1-q) - q(1-p) \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗

    (b) The partial derivative method.

    Use the partial derivative method to find a mixed strategy Nash equilibrium. Which partial derivatives do you set to \(0\text{?}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    • Set \(\frac{\partial \pi_1}{\partial p} = 2 - 3q = 0\) and \(\frac{\partial \pi_2}{\partial q} = 3p - 1 = 0\text{,}\) since each player only controls their own variable.
      🔗
      🔗
    • The equilibrium is \(\left(\frac{1}{3}BB + \frac{2}{3}BF,\ \frac{2}{3}B + \frac{1}{3}F\right)\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (c) Expected payoffs.

    Find each player’s expected payoff at the Nash equilibrium.
    🔗
    Solution.
    Player 1 gets \(\frac{2}{3}\) and Player 2 gets \(-\frac{2}{3}\text{.}\) The payoffs in every cell add to \(0\text{,}\) so \(\pi_2 = -\pi_1\) for every strategy profile.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (d) Interpreting the equilibrium.

    Interpret the equilibrium.
    🔗
    Solution.
    • With the high card, Player 1 always bids.
      🔗
      🔗
    • With the low card, Player 1 bluffs \(\frac{1}{3}\) of the time.
      🔗
      🔗
    • Player 2 bids back \(\frac{2}{3}\) of the time.
      🔗
      🔗
