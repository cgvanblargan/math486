Checkpoint 5.5.1. Interchangeability.

Suppose \((\sigma_1, \sigma_2)\) and \((\tau_1, \tau_2)\) are both Nash equilibria of a two-player zero-sum game. Is \((\sigma_1, \tau_2)\) also a Nash equilibrium?
🔗
  • Yes. By Theorem 5.6, \(\sigma_1\) and \(\tau_2\) are both maximin strategies, so \((\sigma_1, \tau_2)\) is a maximin equilibrium, and therefore a Nash equilibrium.
  • Correct! You can mix and match halves of Nash equilibria in a zero-sum game. By part (2), all of them give the same payoffs, too.
  • There’s no way to tell without seeing the payoffs.
  • Theorem 5.6 answers this for every two-player zero-sum game, no matter what the payoffs are. Which part of the theorem connects Nash equilibria and maximin strategies?
