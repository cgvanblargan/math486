Exercise 5. Hawk and Dove, Revisited.

Return to the Hawk-Dove game of Exercise 2.5.1. Each player chooses hawk (\(h\)) or dove (\(d\)), where \(v\) is the value of the territory and \(w\) is the cost of getting injured.
🔗
In Exercise 2.5.1 we let \(v\) vary. In this exercise we assume \(0 \lt v \lt w\text{,}\) which is the standard assumption for the Hawk-Dove game.
🔗

(a)

Under the assumption \(0 \lt v \lt w\text{,}\) identify any weakly or strictly dominated strategies, or briefly explain why none exist.
🔗
Hint.
Your work on Exercise 2.5.1 is a good place to start.
🔗
🔗
🔗

(b)

Under the assumption \(0 \lt v \lt w\text{,}\) find all Nash equilibria of the game. State each Nash equilibrium as a strategy profile, not just the payoffs it produces.
🔗
🔗

(c)

For each Nash equilibrium you found in part (b), explain informally, in words, why neither animal wants to switch. Write one sentence for each animal, in the context of the fight over territory: what happens to that animal now, and what would happen if it switched on its own while the other animal stayed put.
