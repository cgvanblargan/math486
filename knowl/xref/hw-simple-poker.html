Exercise 3. A Simple Poker Game.

Two players play a simple poker game with a deck of only two cards, a high card (\(H\)) and a low card (\(L\)). The deck is shuffled and one card is dealt to Player 1, so Player 1 is equally likely to get either card. Player 2 does not receive a card.
🔗
  • Player 1 looks at his card and then either bids (\(B\)) or folds (\(F\)). If he folds, the game ends: Player 1 gets \(-2\) and Player 2 gets \(2\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  • If Player 1 bids, Player 2 either bids or folds. She knows Player 1 has bid, but she does not know his card. If she folds, Player 1 gets \(2\) and Player 2 gets \(-2\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  • If both players bid, Player 1 shows his card. With the high card, Player 1 gets \(4\) and Player 2 gets \(-4\text{.}\) With the low card, Player 1 gets \(-4\) and Player 2 gets \(4\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
Player 1’s strategies are \(BB\text{,}\) \(BF\text{,}\) \(FB\text{,}\) and \(FF\text{,}\) where the first letter is what he does with the high card and the second is what he does with the low card. Player 2 only makes one decision, so her strategies are just \(B\) and \(F\text{.}\)
🔗

(a)

Draw the game in extensive form. Start with a node for Nature that determines which card Player 1 gets (high or low), and show Player 2’s information set (connect nodes together with a dotted line if they are in the same information set).
