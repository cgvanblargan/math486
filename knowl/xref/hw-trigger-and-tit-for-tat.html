Exercise 2. Trigger and Tit for Tat.

Players 1 and 2 play the infinitely repeated game whose stage game is the Prisoner’s Dilemma below, with payoffs listed as (Player 1, Player 2).
🔗
The players play this stage game in rounds \(k = 0, 1, 2, \ldots\text{,}\) with discount factor \(\delta\text{,}\) where \(0 \lt \delta \lt 1\text{.}\) If \(p_k\) denotes a player’s payoff in round \(k\text{,}\) then that player’s payoff for the repeated game is
\begin{equation*} \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \delta^k p_k = p_0 + \delta p_1 + \delta^2 p_2 + \cdots. \end{equation*}
🔗
We will use the following strategies for the repeated game:
🔗
  • \(\sigma\text{,}\) the trigger strategy: begin by playing \(c\text{.}\) Keep playing \(c\) as long as the other player plays \(c\text{.}\) If the other player ever plays \(d\text{,}\) play \(d\) in every round after that.
    🔗
    🔗
  • \(\tau\text{,}\) tit for tat: begin by playing \(c\text{.}\) After that, play whatever the other player played in the previous round.
    🔗
    🔗
  • \(\nu\text{,}\) suspicious tit for tat: begin by playing \(d\text{.}\) After that, play whatever the other player played in the previous round.
    🔗
    🔗

(a)

Suppose Player 1 uses \(\sigma\) in the repeated game. Explain in words what this means.

Hint.
The payoffs alternate in sign. A series like \(1 - \delta + \delta^2 - \delta^3 + \cdots\) is still geometric: it has \(a = 1\) and \(r = -\delta\text{,}\) so it equals \(\frac{1}{1 - (-\delta)} = \frac{1}{1 + \delta}\text{.}\) What are \(a\) and \(r\) for each player’s payoffs?
