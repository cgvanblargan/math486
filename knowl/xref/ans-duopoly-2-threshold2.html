Task 1.9.10.b. Firm 2’s threshold.

Find the smallest \(q_1\) for which Firm 2 cannot earn a positive profit.
🔗
Solution.
\(q_1 = 146\text{.}\) On the first piece, leave Firm 2’s profit as revenue minus cost:
🔗
\begin{equation*} \pi_2 = \underbrace{\bigl(150 - q_1 - q_2\bigr)}_{\text{unit price}} q_2 - \underbrace{4}_{\text{unit cost}} q_2 , \end{equation*}
so \(\pi_2 \gt 0\) exactly when the unit price exceeds the unit cost:
🔗
\begin{equation*} 150 - q_1 - q_2 \gt 4 . \end{equation*}
Increasing \(q_2\) only pushes the price down further, so if this fails at \(q_2 = 0\) it fails for every \(q_2 \geq 0\text{.}\) For there to be any range of \(q_2\) at all where Firm 2 profits, the inequality must hold at \(q_2 = 0\text{,}\) which gives the simpler condition
🔗
\begin{equation*} 150 - q_1 \gt 4 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad q_1 \lt 146 . \end{equation*}
So the smallest \(q_1\) ruling out a positive profit entirely is \(q_1 = 146\text{.}\) (On the second piece \(\pi_2 = -4q_2 \leq 0\) regardless, so it never helps.)
