Task 5.7.6.a.

First, suppose each player may only use pure strategies.
🔗
  1. Find each player’s worst case for each of their pure strategies: \(m_1(a)\) and \(m_1(b)\) for Player 1, and \(m_2(x)\) and \(m_2(y)\) for Player 2. For example,
    \begin{equation*} m_1(a) = \min_{\sigma_2} \pi_1(a, \sigma_2). \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
  2. Among pure strategies only, which strategy gives each player the largest worst case? Call these \(s_1\) for Player 1 and \(s_2\) for Player 2. What payoffs \(m_1(s_1)\) and \(m_2(s_2)\) do they guarantee? (This is a “max of mins” like a maximin, but it is not the maximin payoff \(m_1^\dagger\) from Section 5.5, because we’re only allowing pure strategies.)
    🔗
    🔗
  3. Is \((s_1, s_2)\) a Nash equilibrium? Do the two guaranteed payoffs \(m_1(s_1)\) and \(m_2(s_2)\) add up to \(0\text{?}\)
    🔗
    🔗
(Keep your answers in mind. In Exercise 5.7.7, you’ll see what changes when the players can mix.)
🔗
Hint.
For i: the minimum is over all of Player 2’s strategies, including mixed ones. But against a mixed strategy, Player 1’s payoff is a weighted average of her payoffs against \(x\) and \(y\text{,}\) so the worst case is always at a pure strategy.
