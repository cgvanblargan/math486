Section 4.1, Utility Functions and Lotteries.

Read Section 4.1.
🔗
It presents two ideas:
  • A utility function \(u(s)\text{,}\) where \(s\) is an amount of money received. It is increasing (\(u'(s) \gt 0\)) and concave down (\(u''(s) \lt 0\)), so each additional dollar is worth a little less than the one before.
    🔗
    🔗
  • A lottery: outcomes \(x_1, \ldots, x_n\) with probabilities \(p_1, \ldots, p_n\) summing to \(1\text{.}\) Its expected value is
    \begin{equation*} E[x] = p_1 x_1 + \cdots + p_n x_n, \end{equation*}
    and its expected utility is \(E[u(x)] = p_1 u(x_1) + \cdots + p_n u(x_n)\text{.}\)
