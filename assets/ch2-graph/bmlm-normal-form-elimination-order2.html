This time we'll do the elimination in a different order than before, and see that it doesn't finish the same way. At each step, the payoff being compared is highlighted in the columns under discussion.

Comparing Little Monkey's strategies ww and cw: which one dominates the other?
Not quite. Compare Little Monkey's payoff (the second number) in each column, row by row.
Correct.
  • Against Big Monkey's w: cw gives Little Monkey 1, ww gives 0 (1 > 0).
  • Against Big Monkey's c: cw gives Little Monkey 4, ww gives 4 (4 = 4).
Since cw is never worse than ww, and sometimes strictly better, cw weakly dominates ww.

We do the elimination now.

Comparing Little Monkey's strategies wc and cc: which one dominates the other?
Not quite. Compare Little Monkey's payoff (the second number) in each column, row by row.
Correct.
  • Against Big Monkey's w: cc gives Little Monkey 1, wc gives 0 (1 > 0).
  • Against Big Monkey's c: cc gives Little Monkey 3, wc gives 3 (3 = 3).
Since cc is never worse than wc, and sometimes strictly better, cc weakly dominates wc.

We do the elimination now.

Little Monkey now has only cw and cc left. In this reduced 2×2 game, which of Big Monkey's strategies dominates?
Not quite. Compare Big Monkey's payoff (the first number) in the cw and cc columns, row by row.
Correct.
  • In the cw column: w gives Big Monkey 9, c gives 4 (9 > 4).
  • In the cc column: w gives Big Monkey 9, c gives 5 (9 > 5).
Since w is strictly better than c in both remaining columns, w strictly dominates c here.

We do the elimination now.

Only Big Monkey's w is left. In this reduced 1×2 game, does either cw or cc dominate the other for Little Monkey?
Not quite. Compare Little Monkey's payoff (the second number) in the one remaining row.
Correct. With only Big Monkey's w left, cw gives Little Monkey 1 and cc also gives 1 (1 = 1), a tie, and there's nothing else to compare them against. Neither weakly nor strictly dominates the other, so no further elimination is possible.
What remains is a 2×1 game with two strategy profiles left standing: (w, cw) and (w, cc). This ordering shows that w is a dominant strategy for Big Monkey, but it does not pin down a dominant strategy for Little Monkey, unlike the first ordering, which landed on the single profile (w, cw). Same game, same rules, a different order of elimination, a different (and less complete) result.
