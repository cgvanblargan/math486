Little Monkey's first letter is what they do if Big Monkey waits. The second letter is what they do if Big Monkey climbs.

Start at Little Monkey's node after Big Monkey waits. What does Little Monkey choose here?
Not quite. Compare Little Monkey's payoff at the two outcomes below this node.
Correct. Climbing gives Little Monkey 1, waiting gives 0, so Little Monkey climbs.
  • On the tree: the "wait" branch below this node is pruned away.
  • On the matrix: Little Monkey's first letter is what they do if Big Monkey waits. Deciding to climb here rules out every strategy starting with w, so both ww and wc are eliminated (ww is weakly dominated by cw, and wc is weakly dominated by cc).
Now move to Little Monkey's other node, after Big Monkey climbs. What does Little Monkey choose here?
Not quite. Compare Little Monkey's payoff at the two outcomes below this node.
Correct. Waiting gives Little Monkey 4, climbing gives 3, so Little Monkey waits.
  • On the tree: the "climb" branch below this node is pruned away.
  • On the matrix: the second letter is what Little Monkey does if Big Monkey climbs. Deciding to wait here rules out the remaining strategy ending in c, so cc is eliminated (weakly dominated by cw). Little Monkey is left with cw.
Finally, back up to the root. Knowing how Little Monkey will respond, what does Big Monkey choose?
Not quite. Follow each branch down through Little Monkey's remaining choices and compare Big Monkey's payoff.
Correct. Waiting leads to (9, 1) and climbing leads to (4, 4), so Big Monkey waits for a payoff of 9 instead of 4.
  • On the tree: the "climb" branch from the root is pruned away.
  • On the matrix: with only cw left for Little Monkey, row c is dominated by row w, so c is eliminated.
Backward induction on the tree and iterated elimination on the matrix arrived at the same place, one step at a time: Big Monkey waits, Little Monkey plays cw. Every pruning on the tree was an elimination on the matrix. Section 2.12 shows this is not a coincidence.
